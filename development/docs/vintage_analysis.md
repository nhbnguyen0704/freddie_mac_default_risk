## Where this sits in the argument

**This is STEP 4 of four, and this is the window the label uses.**

1. `make vintage-seed` measured a window at the conventional 90+ DPD -- see `docs/vintage_analysis_seed.md`.
2. That window was split into an observation half and an outcome half.
3. `make rollrate` compared the worst rung reached in each half and concluded that bad means **90+ DPD**.
4. **This run** re-measures the curve at that threshold. The window in *The decision* above is measured against the definition the label actually uses, which is what makes it quotable.

> **The window did not move.** Step 1 measured 53 months at 90+ DPD and this run measures the same 53 at 90+ DPD, so the halves the roll rates were read off are the halves this definition implies. The pair (threshold, window) is a fixed point.

## Window options

`captured (%)` is measured the same way the window was chosen: against each cohort's OWN eventual bads, pooled over the cohorts deep enough to have reached that age. `median cohort (%)` is the middle cohort of those, and it lands on the basis by construction -- that is the fixed point checking itself.

`pooled curve would say` is the same capture read off the pooled curve instead. It runs far longer, because the set of cohorts contributing changes with age, so a share of the pooled total tracks the edge of the panel rather than the book. That gap is why the window is not read that way.

## Vintage by origination quarter

The curve above keys every loan-MONTH to the month it was observed in, so a single loan joins many cohorts and the age axis means 'months since we looked'. This one keys each LOAN to the quarter it was ORIGINATED in, and the age axis is the loan's own age. It is the conventional vintage curve, and it answers 'of the loans written in 2021Q3, what share had gone bad by month 18'.

Two differences follow from that, and both matter when reading the two tables against each other:

- **One span per loan, not per loan-month.** Events are TIME TO FIRST here. The observation-month curve is recurrent, because a cured loan genuinely rejoins the population at a fresh observation month; a vintage curve asks when a cohort first deteriorates, so a loan is counted once and stays counted.
- **Left truncation.** 0 of the 27,651,212 loans in the panel were originated before the analysis period. The panel is floored at the period start, so their early ages are simply absent -- we cannot know whether a loan went bad at age 4 when age 4 is not in the data -- and keeping them would count them as having survived ages they were never observed at. They are excluded, leaving **18,249,882 loans** across **40 quarterly cohorts**, of which 263,189 ever reach 90+ DPD.

Origination month is derived as `period - loan_age` rather than joined from the origination table.

## The analysis period

Analysis period: 2016-01-2025-12 (120 observation months), from OBSERVATION_START / OBSERVATION_END. 



The data runs to 2026-03-01, which is 3 month(s) past the last observation month.

| loan-months read         |          rows |
|:-------------------------|--------------:|
| inside 2016-01-2025-12   | 1,390,947,554 |
| after the period         |             0 |
| total opened by this run | 1,431,383,840 |

Months before the period were **never opened**. The floor is applied as partition paths named at read time, so 204 of 327 monthly partitions were left out of the read entirely.

A cohort is a month inside the period.

## The analysed population

944,764 of 28,595,976 loans are held out of this analysis. 27,651,212 remain. 1,407,774 were ever in a workout (modification, payment deferral, forbearance or repayment plan, or principal made non-interest-bearing) and 944,764 were ever delinquent due to a declared disaster. These loans were removed only during the bad definition analysis. These loans were included in the model training with tags of special mentioned events.

## What is in a cohort

A cohort is a reporting month, and a loan belongs to every month it was on the book.

| population                                       |          rows |
|:-------------------------------------------------|--------------:|
| loan-months in the panel                         | 1,320,466,348 |
| cohort memberships (the denominator base)        | 1,303,164,219 |
| dropped -- already past the threshold that month |    17,302,129 |
| distinct loans behind them                       |    27,379,783 |

A loan already past the threshold when the month opens is not a member of that month's cohort.

Events are **recurrent**: a loan that goes bad, cures and relapses rejoins the population the month it cures, and its next default is counted by every cohort that opened while it was performing. Only the months it is actually bad are excluded.

## How to read this

- The **at-risk denominator** is the part people get wrong. Recent cohorts have not had time to season, so a naive share-of-cohort understates their bad rate and flattens the curve. Here the denominator at age *t* is the loans actually observed to age *t*, which is why young vintages stop rather than bend down.
- **Differences in shape between cohorts are the finding**, not noise. They are the argument for out-of-time validation: a model trained on one credit environment and validated randomly across all of them will look better than it is.
- The window is a **decision, not an output**. This file is the evidence for whichever number you put in the label spec's window; if you override it, say why here.
- **The threshold is not decided here.** These curves count a loan bad at 90+ DPD because that is what the roll-rate analysis concluded; this module measures how long to watch, not what to watch for.
